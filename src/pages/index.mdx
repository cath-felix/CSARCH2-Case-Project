<section class="exhibit-section exhibit-intro">
  <h2>Introduction</h2>
  <p>
    When a GPU renders a 3D scene, it needs to apply textures to objects. But what happens when a texture is viewed at different distances or angles? The GPU uses <strong>texture filtering</strong> to determine pixel colors — and two of the most fundamental methods are <strong>Nearest Neighbor</strong> and <strong>Bilinear Filtering</strong>.
  </p>
</section>

<div class="exhibit-divider"></div>

<div class="exhibit-two-col">
  <section class="exhibit-panel exhibit-panel--dark">
    <h2>What is Texture Filtering?</h2>
    <div class="exhibit-panel__body">
      <div class="exhibit-panel__text">
        <p>
          Texture filtering is the process of <strong>sampling a texture image</strong> and calculating the correct color for each pixel on screen. It is a critical GPU function that affects visual quality, performance, and memory usage.
        </p>
        <p>
          When a coordinate does not lie on texel centers, the Texture Mapping Unit (TMU) must interpolate color values — often while fetching texels from cache and VRAM.
        </p>
      </div>

    <h2>Pixels vs. Texels</h2>
    <div class="exhibit-panel__body exhibit-panel__body--stacked">
      <div class="exhibit-panel__text">
        <p>
          A <strong>pixel</strong> is a single dot on your screen. A <strong>texel</strong> is a single dot in a texture image. One screen pixel does not always map to one texel — especially when zooming or viewing surfaces at an angle.
        </p>
        <p>
          When a texture is displayed larger or smaller than its original size, the GPU must <strong>interpolate</strong> to calculate missing pixel colors.
        </p>

<section class="exhibit-section exhibit-nearest">
  <h2>Nearest Neighbor Filtering</h2>
  <p class="exhibit-lead">
    The GPU picks the closest texel to the sample point. No averaging, no blending. Formula: <code>P(x,y) = P(round(x), round(y))</code>
  </p>

  <div class="exhibit-pros-cons">
    <div class="exhibit-pros-cons__col">
      <h3>Advantages</h3>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Fastest</strong> filtering method</li>
        <li>Simple to implement</li>
        <li>Preserves sharp edges</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
    <div class="exhibit-pros-cons__col">
      <h3>Disadvantages</h3>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Blocky</strong>, pixelated appearance when zoomed in</li>
        <li>Noticeable aliasing (jagged edges)</li>
        <li>Poor quality for realistic images</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
  </div>

<section class="exhibit-section exhibit-bilinear">
  <h2>Bilinear Filtering</h2>
  <div class="exhibit-bilinear-grid">
    <div class="exhibit-bilinear-text">
      <p>
        The GPU averages the four nearest texels (a 2×2 grid) and blends them together. Formula: <code>P(x,y) = lerp(lerp(T00,T10,fx), lerp(T01,T11,fx), fy)</code>
      </p>
      <p>
        Bilinear filtering is more accurate and more common in modern graphics hardware. It produces smoother gradients than nearest neighbor, though textures can appear slightly blurry when magnified.
      </p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Smooth</strong> appearance with no obvious pixelation</li>
        <li>Slower than nearest neighbor, but still hardware-accelerated</li>
        <li>Used in most modern games and 3D applications</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
    <figure class="exhibit-figure">

<section class="exhibit-section exhibit-supplementary">
  <h2>Comparison Table</h2>

| Feature | Nearest Neighbor | Bilinear |
|---------|------------------|----------|
| **Quality** | Low (blocky) | Medium (smooth) |
| **Performance** | Fastest | Good |
| **GPU Usage** | Very Low | Low |
| **Formula** | Closest texel | Blend of 4 texels |

  <h2>When to Use Each Method</h2>

| Scenario | Recommended Method | Why |
|----------|-------------------|-----|
| **Pixel art games** | Nearest Neighbor | Preserves blocky aesthetic |
| **Retro style games** | Nearest Neighbor | Authentic retro look |
| **Modern 3D games** | Bilinear | Good balance of quality/speed |
| **VR/AR applications** | Anisotropic | Best quality for angles |
| **Mobile games** | Bilinear | Performance-focused |
